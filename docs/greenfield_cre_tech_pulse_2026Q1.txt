1. MARKET BACKDROP: OFFICE DISTRESS MEETS LENDER SCRUTINY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
US office vacancy ended 2025 at 19.6%, a new high, while retail and industrial vacancy were stable at 6.1% and 7.4% respectively. Roughly $310 billion of CRE debt matures in 2026, and refinancing remains difficult for older office assets. Lenders are asking borrowers for better, more frequent lease-level reporting — rent rolls, option schedules, co-tenancy clauses — which is one reason demand for lease data tools has held up despite pressure on operator budgets.

The flip side: mid-market operators are cutting discretionary technology spend. In our Q4 2025 survey of 120 operators, 41% said they expect to consolidate software vendors in 2026, and 33% said procurement now reviews software purchases above $50,000 that previously sat with asset management teams.

3. THE AI LEASE ABSTRACTION RACE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Two years ago, AI abstraction was a differentiator. Today, almost every vendor in the category claims it. Leading vendors now report field-level accuracy of 97% or more on standard leases — though methodologies vary widely and few are third-party audited. In our conversations with buyers, three things now separate vendors:

  (a) Performance on hard documents — ground leases, heavily amended leases, poor scans.
  (b) Workflow and audit trail — can the operator show a lender where each number came from?
  (c) Data rights — operators, and increasingly their lenders, want assurance that lease data is not used to train models for other customers.

Open-weight models have narrowed the gap between start-ups and established vendors. One CTO told us: "The model is maybe 20% of the value now. The other 80% is the review workflow and the integrations."

4. WHAT BUYERS ARE ASKING IN DILIGENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
From our recent sell-side processes, the most common diligence requests in vertical SaaS are:
- ARR definitions: whether one-time fees, signed-not-live contracts or pilots are counted.
- NRR methodology: twelve-month cohort basis versus annualised recent quarters.
- Year-end cohort quality: discounting and pull-forward ahead of a sale process.
- AI claims: benchmark methodology, share of human review, model ownership and training-data rights.
- Customer concentration after large expansions.
